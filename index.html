Private Label Products From China: Samples, Artwork, MOQ and Packaging Before Production

Buying ready-stock goods from China is mostly a purchasing problem. Building a private label product is a project. You are asking a supplier to change materials, add a logo, print packaging and hit a launch date, and every one of those changes can move the MOQ, the cost and the risk. JTLGO, a China sourcing and DDP logistics service, helps DTC, Amazon, retail and wholesale buyers develop branded product lines, and its private label sourcing page is built around one warning: do not approve production from unclear samples or incomplete packaging artwork.

Start with a brief a supplier can actually quote

A private label quote is only as good as the brief behind it. JTLGO asks buyers to prepare two groups of information.

The product side: your product idea, reference photos, functional requirements, target retail price, quantity and launch timeline. The brand side: logo, artwork, packaging dieline, barcode, carton marks, material preferences and compliance expectations.

With that in hand, the China-side questions become concrete: does the supplier have real development capability, what is the sample process, what MOQ applies, which material options exist, and what production lead time can they commit to? JTLGO's page on Alibaba private label sourcing adds customisation scope and inspection requirements to the list. A supplier chosen only on unit price, while ignoring MOQ, sample quality and production consistency, is the second risk the site highlights.

Sample the product and the packaging separately

Private label buyers often approve the product sample and forget that the packaging is a separate sample with its own supplier, materials and minimums. JTLGO's guidance is to verify sample details, packaging, barcode and carton plan before production.

For the product, record a measurable approval: material, dimensions and tolerances, colour reference, function and the packed unit. For the packaging, check artwork against the dieline, print colour, barcode placement and legibility, and whether the box protects the product in transit. Give each approved version a reference number and date. JTLGO's samples-to-bulk checklist explains why a later change, such as a new lining or a different print, should reset the relevant approval.

Packaging decisions change MOQ, damage risk and freight cost

JTLGO puts it directly: packaging decisions affect MOQ, damage risk, dimensional weight and landed cost. A printed retail box may carry its own minimum from the printer even when the factory accepts a small product run. A larger, more premium box adds volumetric weight, which matters because air freight is charged on the higher of actual and volumetric weight.

Ask suppliers to quote alternatives side by side, for example custom printed packaging versus plain packaging with a label, so you can see what the brand experience costs before you commit. JTLGO's MOQ negotiation guide lists plain packaging instead of a dedicated print run as one of the levers worth pricing.

Plan labels and carton marks for where the goods will go

Where the goods end up changes what the cartons need. Retail buyers may need specific carton marks; Amazon sellers need FNSKU and carton labels that match the shipment plan. JTLGO's Amazon FBA prep service covers warehouse intake, basic QC photos, SKU separation, FNSKU and carton-label checks, standard repacking and recorded carton data. Decide this before production so the factory can pack correctly the first time rather than relabelling in the warehouse.

Compliance belongs in the same conversation. Destination requirements and model-specific documents depend on the product and market, and JTLGO advises adding qualified testing for regulated or safety-critical items.

Timing is worth planning in the same way. Artwork approval, packaging printing and product production often run in parallel, and a late packaging decision can hold finished goods at the factory. Ask the supplier which approvals must be complete before production starts, and keep a single written list of the approved product version, the approved packaging version and the agreed carton plan. When a reorder comes, that list is what the next batch is checked against, so the second production run matches the first without relying on anyone's memory.

Inspect before dispatch, then quote the route

When production is finished, compare goods against the approved references before they leave China. JTLGO's warehouse takes basic QC photos and videos on receipt, which can reveal visible differences in product, label or packaging. The site notes that these do not replace lab testing or a full factory inspection, so define a wider scope through its quality inspection service when the order value or product risk justifies it.

Only then is the freight quote meaningful. JTLGO's advice is to collect packed carton data as early as possible and ask for route review before committing to payment, pickup or door delivery. Final pricing uses real carton count, packed dimensions, weight, cargo description and destination scope. For duty-paid delivery, the DDP shipping page explains what the route can include.

FAQ

What do I need before asking for a private label quote?

Product idea, reference photos, functions, target retail price, quantity and timeline, plus logo, artwork, dieline, barcode, carton marks and compliance expectations.

Why is packaging MOQ different from product MOQ?

Printed packaging often comes from a separate supplier with its own minimum, so it can stay high even when the factory accepts fewer units.

Can JTLGO prepare private label goods for Amazon FBA?

Yes. Its FBA prep service includes SKU separation, FNSKU and carton-label checks, standard repacking and carton data.

Do warehouse photos prove the product meets standards?

No. JTLGO states that basic photo checks do not replace lab testing or full factory inspection.
